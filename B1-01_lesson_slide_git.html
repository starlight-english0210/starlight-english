Breaking the Ice

Greetings & Small Talk — 50-min lesson

Today's Key Expressions
How's it going?
What have you been up to?
📌 TODAY'S GOAL
By the end of today's lesson, you will be able to:
  • Open a conversation naturally with someone you haven't seen in a while
  • Ask what someone has been up to recently
  • Respond naturally and show genuine interest
  • Close the chat gracefully using target expressions
📘 Section 1 · Expression Check

Key Expressions

1. Opening a Conversation
A) For someone you see regularly
How's it going?
Casual all-purpose greeting
How are things?
Slightly warmer, invites a real answer
B) For someone you haven't seen in a while
How have you been?
Genuine check-in after time apart
Long time no see!
Friendly exclamation
It's been a while!
Same feeling, slightly softer
2. Asking What Someone Has Been Doing
What have you been up to?
Most natural — "what have you been doing lately?"
Anything new with you?
Light — no pressure to give a full answer
How did [event] go?
Follow-up when you knew about something
"We haven't spoken in weeks. What have you been up to?"
3. Responding Naturally
Not much, just...
Nothing big to report
Actually, quite a lot!
You have news to share
It's been pretty hectic.
You've been very busy
4. Wrapping Up Small Talk
Anyway, I should get going.
Polite exit
It was great catching up!
Warm close after a longer chat
Let's catch up properly soon.
Suggests a longer conversation later

VOCABULARY NOTE
get the hang of something
To learn how to do something through practice until it feels natural.
～のコツをつかむ、慣れる
a permanent fixture
A person who is always present in a particular place; a familiar, regular face.
常連、その場に欠かせない存在
Kevin
How's it going so far, Mai?
Mai
Pretty well! I'm still getting used to the espresso machine, though.
Kevin
Ha, everyone struggles with it at first. You'll get the hang of it.
Charlotte
(walks in) Kevin! Long time no see!
Kevin
Charlotte! It's been a while. What have you been up to?
Charlotte
I've been traveling for work — just got back from Hokkaido yesterday. Four cities in four days! Oh — who's this?
Kevin
This is Mai, our new part-timer. Mai, this is Charlotte — she's basically a permanent fixture here.
Mai
Nice to meet you! I'm Mai.
Charlotte
Welcome to Bridge! You're going to love it here.

Aileen and Sameer run into each other at Bridge Café on Thursday afternoon. They haven't spoken since Sameer's big project.
Aileen
Sameer! How have you been? It's been a while.
Sameer
Aileen! Great to see you. Honestly, it's been pretty hectic — we had a major system rollout last week.
Aileen
Oh wow. How did that go?
Sameer
Better than expected, actually. A few late nights, but the team pulled through. What about you? Anything new?
Aileen
Actually, quite a lot! We just signed a deal with a company in Germany. I've been preparing for our first online meeting with their global team.
Sameer
That's exciting! Is the meeting in English?
Aileen
Yes, all in English. No pressure at all.
Sameer
You'll be great. You always are.
Aileen
Thank you. Anyway, I should get going — I have a 3 o'clock. But let's catch up properly soon!
Sameer
Absolutely. Good luck with the meeting!

🗣 Section 6 · Express Yourself

Express Yourself

⏱ Target 12 min

Situation: "Catching Up"

You run into a classmate or colleague you haven't seen for about a month at a convenience store. You have a few minutes to chat before you both need to head off.

Try to:
  • Open naturally — don't just say "Hello"
  • Ask at least one follow-up question to show genuine interest
  • Use one expression from today to wrap up the chat
📝 YOUR IDEAS (from HW)
Think about the conversation you would have and write down your ideas below.
Conversation Starter
A: Oh! (Partner's name)!
B: Wow! (Partner's name)!   .
EXAMPLE STARTERS FOR STUDENTS
"Oh! I haven't seen you in ages. How's it going?"
"What have you been up to lately? You seem so busy these days."
"It's been a while! How are things? I heard you had a big deadline last week."
"Hey! I was just thinking about you the other day. What have you been up to?"
💡 Section 6 · Useful Expressions

Useful Words & Expressions

Extra vocabulary for Express Yourself

USEFUL EXPRESSIONS
Speak of the devil!
Said when someone appears just as you were talking about them.
噂をすれば！
e.g. "Oh, speak of the devil! We were just talking about you!"
What a small world!
Said when you unexpectedly meet someone you know.
こんなところで会うなんて、世界は狭いね。
e.g. "I ran into my old teacher at the airport. What a small world!"
We keep missing each other.
We have had difficulty finding a time to meet.
すれ違いが続いてるね。
e.g. "We keep missing each other! Let's actually set a date this time."

🎭 Section 7 · Try It Out

Old Friends Reunion

⏱ Target 10 min

Situation: "Old Friends"

You unexpectedly run into someone you haven't seen in over a year — maybe an old classmate, a former colleague, or a neighbor who moved away. You only have about 10 minutes before you each need to leave.

Try to include all four:
  • An opener that fits the long time apart
  • A question about what they've been doing
  • A genuine reaction to something they share
  • A natural way to end the conversation
Conversation Starter
A: Oh my goodness, (your partner's name)!
B: Wow! Is that you, (your partner's name)?  .
EXAMPLE STARTERS FOR STUDENTS
"Oh my goodness! I can't believe it! It's been over a year! How have you been?"
"No way! Long time no see! What have you been up to?"
"I was literally just wondering how you were doing! It's been forever — how's everything?"
"I've been meaning to reach out for months. How have you been? You look great!"
💡 Section 7 · Useful Expressions

Useful Words & Expressions

Extra vocabulary for Try It Out

USEFUL EXPRESSIONS
lose touch
To gradually stop communicating with someone.
疎遠になる、連絡が途絶える
e.g. "We lost touch after university, but I ran into her last year."
I've been meaning to reach out.
I had been intending to contact you.
連絡しようと思っていたんだけど。
e.g. "I've been meaning to reach out — I heard you moved to a new city!"
You haven't changed a bit!
You look exactly the same as before.
全然変わってないね！
e.g. "Oh my gosh, you haven't changed a bit! How long has it been?"
Let's not let that happen again.
We should make sure we stay in touch from now on.
またこんなに間が空かないようにしよう。
e.g. "It was so good to see you. Let's not let that happen again!"
