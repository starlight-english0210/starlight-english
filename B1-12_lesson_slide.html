Doing or To Do?

enjoy doing / decide to do / want someone to do — 50-min lesson

Today's Grammar Focus
verb + -ing  /  verb (+ someone) + to …
The first verb decides the form of the second verb.
📌 TODAY'S GOAL
By the end of today's lesson, you will be able to:
  • Choose -ing or to … after common verbs (enjoy doing / decide to do)
  • Say what you want others to do with want / ask / tell + someone + to …
  • Talk about rules with make / let + someone + do
📘 Section 1 · Grammar Focus

Doing or To Do?

1. Verb + -ing
I really enjoyed talking with you.
enjoy / mind / suggest / avoid / finish + -ing
✗ I enjoyed to talk
I keep forgetting his name.
give up (= stop) / put off (= delay)
keep (on) (= continue) + -ing
I enjoy not having to commute.
Negative = not + -ing
2. Verb + to …
I've decided to quit my job.
decide / plan / hope / promise / agree / refuse + to …
I can't afford to buy a new car.
manage = succeed (after some difficulty)
afford = have enough money / time
I promised not to tell anyone.
Negative = not to …
3. Verb + someone + to …
Do you want me to help you?
want / would like / expect + someone + to …
✗ Do you want that I help you?
Can you remind me to call Sam?
ask / tell / remind / advise / allow + someone + to …
Let me know if you need anything.
make / let + someone + do
No "to"! ✗ made me to work
💡 suggest is different: ✗ He suggested me to go. → He suggested going.

1. Charlotte has decided to take the Japanese test in .

VOCABULARY NOTE
JLPT
The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test. N5 is the easiest level and N1 is the hardest.
日本語能力試験
persuade someone to ~
To make someone agree to do something by giving them good reasons.
（人）を説得して～させる
I know the feeling.
I understand, because the same thing has happened to me.
その気持ち、よくわかる
give it a try
To try doing something to see if it works.
試しにやってみる
Deal!
Used to say "I agree" to an offer or promise.
決まり！、それで行こう！
Sameer
Charlotte, you look exhausted. Have you been writing late again?
Charlotte
Not this time. I've decided to take the JLPT in December, so I've been studying every night.
Sameer
Good for you! Which level?
Charlotte
N3. My Japanese teacher persuaded me to try it. Honestly, I almost gave up studying kanji last month.
Sameer
I know the feeling. When I first came to Japan, I kept forgetting the ones I'd learned the week before.
Charlotte
Really? But you passed N1! Any tips?
Sameer
Well, my first teacher told me to write a short diary in Japanese every day. And I avoided using the dictionary on my phone.
Charlotte
That sounds tough, but I'll give it a try. Would you mind checking my diary sometimes?
Sameer
Not at all! But only if you promise not to give up this time.
Charlotte
Deal!

Aileen stops by Bridge after work. She wants to hear about something important Mai did last week.
Aileen
Mai! How did the interview go? I've been dying to hear.
Mai
Honestly, better than I expected! Charlotte's practice interview really helped. I managed to answer every question in English.
Aileen
That's fantastic! What did they ask you?
Mai
They asked me to describe a time I solved a problem at work, so I talked about Bridge. The interviewer said she enjoyed hearing about it.
Aileen
Perfect example. Did anything go wrong?
Mai
Well, at one point I couldn't stop saying "um." And they wanted me to do a short role-play with a "guest" — I almost forgot to smile!
Aileen
Ha! Everyone gets nervous. So when will you hear back?
Mai
They promised to let me know by the end of the month. I keep checking my email every five minutes!
Aileen
Try to avoid thinking about it too much. Whatever happens, you should be proud of yourself.
Mai
Thanks, Aileen. If I get it, I'll let you know first!

🗣 Section 6 · Express Yourself

Express Yourself

⏱ Target 12 min

Situation: "What's New with You?"

You run into a friend you haven't seen for a few months. They ask how life has been. Tell them about the habits you have changed recently — and something you want to try next.

Try to:
  • Talk about something new you have started recently
  • Talk about something you have stopped, or are trying to stop
  • Say who or what gave you the idea
  • Share a goal or plan for the next few months
📝 YOUR IDEAS (from HW)
Think about the conversation you would have and write down your ideas below.
Conversation Starter
A: Long time no see! So, what's new with you?
B: Well, quite a lot, actually!  .
EXAMPLE STARTERS FOR STUDENTS
"I've started going to a yoga class on Saturday mornings, and I really enjoy it."
"I finally managed to stop eating snacks late at night. It took me three tries!"
"My kids persuaded me to get a dog, so now I walk him twice a day."
"I keep telling myself to read more, but I always end up watching videos instead."
"I'm planning to take a cooking class this winter. I want to learn to make curry from scratch."
🎭 Section 7 · Try It Out

Growing Up

⏱ Target 10 min

Situation: "How Strict Were Your Parents?"

You and a friend are chatting about your childhoods. Your friend says their parents were really strict. Compare it with how you grew up — the rules at home, the freedom you had, and what your parents hoped for you.

Try to include all four:
  • A rule you had to follow at home
  • Something you were — or weren't — free to do
  • Something your parents hoped you would do or become
  • How you feel about it now, looking back
Conversation Starter
A: My parents were so strict when I was a kid. What about yours?
B: Really? Mine were  . For example,  .
EXAMPLE STARTERS FOR STUDENTS
"My parents made me practice the piano every day, and I hated it at the time."
"They didn't let me have a phone until high school, so I always felt left out."
"My mother wanted me to become a doctor, but I decided to study art instead."
"We had a strict curfew — I had to be home by seven, even in high school!"
"Looking back, I'm really glad they encouraged me to read so much."
